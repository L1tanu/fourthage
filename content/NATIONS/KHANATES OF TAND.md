# PEREGRINE: THE KHANATES OF TAND

Far to the west, where the Throne of Ensas rose and fell a millennium ago, the Khanates of Tand wage war against the gods themselves. Having endured the turn of Ages, the Khanates are feared throughout the Alliance of Gray Marches as an eternal force of chaos and change; it was only by the express approval of the east-most Khans--the ones bordering Marathas--that Deiji Khatun could ascend to the role of Supreme Commander. An act of open defiance against their battle-wise neighbors, the tribune reasoned, would’ve numbered the days of the new Marathan confederacy.

Yet as threatening as the Khanates first appeared, they have so far proven stalwart defenders of the Alliance against the tides of western aggression: legions of Nadrii, Khazimir, and the Old Throne have marched on the valleys of Tand only to disappear entirely, their passage unmarked and whereabouts unknown. “What is given to the trackless steppes,” the men of Tand say, “is not returned to the annals of history.”

The eminent realms of Tand are known throughout the Alliance of Gray Marches; further beyond in the southern reaches of Montclaive or Vemaire, where the ancient Hordes of Tand were summoned by the Marathans to conquer pre-Tousavi lands, the modern Khanates are respected as close cultural contemporaries. The east-most territories, and thereby the closest to the Gray Marches, are **NULYAMÜL, THE WEEPING MOUNTAIN; ORGALI, THE SALT REACHES; and KRO ÜURHAI, THE BLACK CANYON.** 
## Tav-Om, the Five Truths
Whenever something becomes so powerful as to uphold a pillar of the earth--be it a force of nature or institution--it loses its own identity and becomes wholly incapable of goodness, which is enacted by individuals to other individuals. It is a commonly held belief within Tand that the mightiest gods are not merely arbiters of our realm, but our opponents within it. The difference between mortal and god, the wisemen argue, is the spiritually corruptive nature of power: the ocean, in its abundance, loses its ability to be a moral actor; likewise, the sun’s scorching rays are not anymore good than they are evil; the harsh steppe winds are breath, but the storms they stir unravel entire civilizations; it goes on. 

Humanity, as naturally tumultuous creatures who possess a conscience, are thought to be not of fire, water, wind, or earth, but breath. This is a natural force unique to men, through which we become capable of shaping the elemental world towards moral ends: we irrigate with the waters, build great monuments of stone, travel with the wind, and smith with fire. World-shaping miracles such as these cannot manifest outside the hands of mankind.

These doctrines are thin and spread wide across the Khanates, in some places it is mere superstition and in others ever-present dogma. Broadly this system of beliefs is called the “Tav-Om,” or the “Five Truths,” for the five mightiest cosmic forces which the men of Tand recognize: fire that purifies, sky that witnesses, water that connects, earth that defines, and breath that transforms. 

Oftentimes these five forces are seen as divine kingdoms or spiritual planes, each ruled by an individual overgod under which innumerable lesser spirits dwell. These are Nu-Beye, Sun-Father; Nu-Shui, Ocean-Mother; Nu-Shuvuu, Wind-Father; and Nu-Tavstaan, Earth-Father. The final kingdom, the realm of breath, is without a singular ruler. Instead, it is ruled over by a host of lesser gods, referred to as the Sul.
## The Mortal Gods and the Küryomen
Unique amongst the realms of the gods are the lesser spirits known as Sul, neither purely man nor divine, but a mixture which possesses the qualities of both. They are “mortal gods,” who lack any form of physical power and are therefore seen as incapable of doing harm. It is through the guidance of the mortal gods that men first learned to tame the elements, wielding the primordial Theurgic and Thaumaturgic powers that enabled the rise of Ensas two millennia ago. Since then the men of Tand have revered the mortal gods’ sage knowledge as the most righteous tool of cultivation.

The Sul are said to inhabit all things: when an item or idea exists long enough in the presence of mankind, it is ensouled by their collective breath. This “breath soul” is then nurtured, much like earth is shaped or water is channeled, into a form of divinity. A Sul can be a place, a person, or an idea. Some patron Sul are a combination of all three, or defy categorization altogether; what unifies them is that inability to inflict suffering, and the capacity to die.

If forgotten, abandoned, or dispossessed, a Sul may fade, their breath soul spent, and experience a spiritual death. It is therefore the job of the Küryomen--wisepeople of Tand--to religiously document the names of the mortal gods, to provide them sacrifice, and spread their stories through oral tradition (it is believed they live by breath, and so the true shape of them can only be articulated through speech) until through the cooperative effort of generations the mortal gods are made quasi-immortal. As idols to rival the four overgods.

In the days of Ensas, these Küryomen were distinct entities within a tribal or familial unit, oftentimes selected for the vocation by fits of turmoil thought to be spurred by the mortal gods. Over the hundreds of years since then, however, the role has shifted, mutated, and ultimately transformed into being indistinguishable with the function of a Khan: both ruler and holyman, soul-namer and king.

While there are no universal symbols of the Tav-Om, objects that have cultivated immense spiritual energy are believed to become holy relics of the Sul. They are highly varied and oftentimes incredibly personalized, but are typically family heirlooms. Because of this, items passed down from one’s parents may house a breath soul nurtured by generations of their ancestors.
## Gallu, the Sons of Suls
The khans, or küryomen, undergo extensive physical and mental training in their youth before they are chosen to ascend to the rank. On the eve of their coronation, they are ritually sacrificed through ceremonial rites so precise and so esoteric as to always, without fail, produce the exact same appearance of Gallu. 

This act of ritual suicide is thought to offer the body--the flesh soul, discussed later--as a dowry to their family’s patron Sul. If the Sul accepts it (which they nearly always do), the khan is resurrected, here called a youn, with the mutated marks of their patron Sul now branded upon them. To bear these curses is considered both a great honor and a sign of devotion, as it binds their first life and first death to their clan’s chief Sul.

Should the ritual somehow fail and the youn return marked by an unknown Sul, the Gallu are called ezengüi--the ownerless, or Sons of Wandering Suls--and summarily exiled so as not to offend their patron Sul. Many clans of nomads which wander the plains of Tand claim to have roots in these unfortunate occurrences, tracing their lineage from ancestors condemned to a life of isolated survival.
## The Structure of Souls
Before one can grasp the structure of society in Tand, they must first understand the Khanates’ conception of the human form. It is a vessel split three ways: the flesh, the star, the breath; each said to bear a ‘soul’, a kind of spiritual essence which transcends the physical. 

The flesh soul is the exterior, the thing that is shaped, and changes constantly throughout our life. In the ritual to create a Youn, the flesh soul is traded as a form of currency.

The star soul lies beyond the reach of the flesh, determined at birth. It is said those born under the radiance of the sun have a rising star soul, representing nobility, virtue, and divinity, or under the moon as a falling star soul, denoting mundanity, passivity, and simplicity. Only through the rite of the youn can one have their star soul changed by remaking the flesh under a different light.

The breath soul is unique to humanity, it is the combined energy of thought and action that becomes imbued into objects. As the only creators of breath, humans naturally would act as vessels brimming with it at their birth. A man blessed by a Sul, still full of breath, will be reformed in its image. The breath soul has no shape, simply filling to match its container.
## The Organization of Peoples
The division of peoples into two aspects--the rising star souls and the falling star souls--began sometime in Ensas and has endured into the modern day, albeit in a form so distinct as to be nigh-unrecognizable from its origins. Now there are two castes called the khartsa and khitso.

The khartsa broadly encompasses any non-noble persons. Elevation within this aspect is achieved by embodying a certain archetype:
- Judge-arbiters whose knowledge of custom impresses others.
- Improvisational bards whose satire is respected. 
- Warriors of merit who regale bloody tales of conquest in battle.
These kinds of people are largely aristocrats or courtiers whose verbal prowess earns them acclaim. Because of an emphasis on the power of the breath soul, eloquence is prized as one of the few ways to ascend in society.

Then the khitso are born of noteworthy lineages or remade as youn. This caste is conferred only through descendancy, and so is composed entirely of princes, nobles, or lords. There is no way for a khartsa to become a khitso except for the rite of youn rebirth.

At the highest echelon of the khisto is the khan, nowadays also the küryomen, who are self-styled “Arms of the Mortal Gods.” They are called khan, khatun, or khagan, and rule over their respective territories with absolute authority, often with the aid of a council of khitso--some of whom are themselves youn.
## Noteworthy Khanates
### BORDER KHANATES (Nulyamül, Orgali):
The Weeping Mountain sits at the fulcrum of three kingdoms, spearing the border of Evvel Aytzel, New Marathas, and Vemaire. In its long shadow is a cold steppe which stretches to the salt rivers of the Elyon Sea, where the riverside fishing villages of Orgali dot an expanse of marshes. So close to the core of the Marathan Empire, the men of Nulyamül have been called on throughout history for their legendary prowess in battle. It is said that in the shade of the Weeping Mountain there is no word for “soldier.” The word for man suffices.

A strict honor-culture governs the clans of Nulyamül, who regularly gather in a war summit of both khitso and khartsa under the banner of the Khan Aimshai Perenlei. In some cases the non-noble khartsa are more highly prized than their khisto counterpart, as their experience in combat or diplomatic abilities are seen as truer manifestations of the Suls’ guidance. This has led to them appearing decadent or lax in the eyes of the other khanates, who believe the boundaries between khitso and khartsa are absolute and should be respected, keeping with tradition.

The clans of Orgali consider themselves the most learned and wizened of Tand’s khanates, in part due to a strong relationship with the schoolmen of Ansium, who regularly consult the küryomen on matters of religious history. Because of this, it is common to see sage-soldiers who are trained in high mountain monasteries, proudly wearing their Thaumaturgic scars as a mark of pride. When slaying a foe or surviving a great battle, it is customary for the warriors of Orgali to scar their arms with sorvan, ritual markings meant to imitate the sacrifices sorcerers make and imply the authority of otherworldly might.
**Suggested Trades:** Master-at-Arms, Monk, Wiseman, Battlemage, Warrior, Herder, Fisher, Salt-Trader, Mercenary, Falconer.
**Male Names:** Tömörbaatar, Batbayar, Sechen, Bilgüün, Davaa, Ganbaatar, Gerel, Temüülen, Yeke, Aimshai.
**Female Names:** Oyuun, Tsetsegmaa, Erdem, Mönkhzayaa, Sarülem, Sarangerel, Ariun, Zaya, Dölgöön, Altantsetseg.
### COASTAL KHANATE (Kro Üurhai):
The Black Canyons of Kro Üurhai are considered by its inhabitants to be tamed desolation. Its coastal lands are blighted by ash, spewed forth from the Marathan island of Eki Kay’s volcano, which covers the plains between ravines which gouge so deep into the earth that entire civilizations are thought to have been lost to their depths. These dizzying extremes are where the residents of Kro Üurhai thrive: they are expert survivalists who pray to mortal gods of the Second Age, priding themselves on their abilities to endure any calamity, or fell any foe.

Great horned, woolly beasts roam the wastes here on the border of Nadrii and the Khanates. By their residents they are called the avarga--devilkin to all others--who were believed to have been herded by the giants of Ensas in a time when the clades of men were more distinct. Taller than ten men and wider than twenty, the avarga are feared as unkillable demons of the ashen plains; only the Üurhaians are capable of hunting them.

Tradition holds that the Üurhaians are extremely concerned with mastery over the elements. The ability to call down rain, or to make fertile unlivable grounds, are coveted blessings here more than anywhere else in the Khanates, where some semblance of normalcy may be clutched to. Unique to the Üurhaians are the Ar’i-Saruh, the Tamers of All Things, who specialize in both Theurgy and Thaumaturgy in a dualistic form of casting which is said to draw on both the breath soul and flesh soul. Many tribal nobles of this realm boast immense magical prowess as a symbol of their station. Here, one’s mastery defines one’s status.
**Suggested Trades:** Apothecary, Diviner, Hunter, Master-at-Arms, Miner, Scout, Warrior, Witch
**Male Names:** Kharankhui, Tergel, Zorig, Ulaan, Temür, Nergüi, Batmönkh, Arslan, Oghuz, Yesügei.
**Female Names:** Gal, Temüjin-e, Uuriintuya, Khavtgai, Ekhnii, Setgel, Khulan, Öngön, Bayan. 
### The Nomads
Between the realms of Tand exist bands of nomads, ensconced in the steppe hills and serving as the connective tissue between them. They are, for the most part, their own independent and mobile nation-states that operate irrespective of the other Khanates, choosing to defy the traditional concepts of borders in favor of “riding the wind.” What this means in practice is that they serve as the chains binding the clans of Tand: nomads deliver essential goods between the Khanates while producing a great many themselves, garnering deep reverence and respect from their kinsmen.

The origins of the nomads vary from tribe to tribe. The Younmen of Nulyamül believe themselves to be descendants of the Ensanese emperor’s brother, the Nuhba: the story goes that the Nuhba engaged his emperor-brother in a contest for the throne. Their wager went that whichever of them could reach his arm into a pit of flame and not flinch from its heat would be awarded stewardship of the earth; as the story goes, the Nuhba was mutilated by their challenge, transforming him into the first and greatest Gallu who was condemned to walk alone.

Another tribe, the Tengti-Sul of Orgali, assert that they are the mortal incarnation of the Sul. Curiously, this group is capable of a unique form of chanted magic bestowed by an unknown god, in which they speak a mystical dialect of Voidtongue that is purely breath control. It is rumored they drift from place to place, walking hidden roads of the realm that exists between heaven and earth.

Perhaps the most well-known tribe is that of Kro Üurhai’s Süükhud, who are traders that frequent the other Khanates to hawk wares from the far kingdoms of the west. Market days are a fixture where they travel, and people will gather from all over to barter for baubles unavailable anywhere else. This tribe is paid the utmost respect, and discourteous behavior towards them is highly taboo. Offending the Süükhud might mean months without luxury goods. Therefore a premium is placed on immediate conflict resolution--a mindset which extends to most Khanates’ laws, which seeks to settle disputes against the nomads within the day they have been committed.
