*"The Sisters of Agharti were cruel to their dying. That was the common misconception, and my immediate thought upon seeing her throat slit. She was a spry woman in her twentieth summer. Days earlier we had shared a meal and spoken at length of the Yiegdra sea. She had never left the convent and harbored whimsical fantasies of great serpents, of continental tempests. On the third night of her fever, no closer to breaking than the first, the Sisters killed her in her bed. Her neck was cut from shoulder to shoulder. A dark, herbal poultice was placed in her mouth, fixed in a rictus. Her eyes were stitched open. Her shoes were laced tight. The cloudy-eyed Abbess explained this as the Sisters did their work. These measures were essential for her journey home. I recall not thinking so much as observing. When her body was moved into the central cistern, doted over, whispered encouragement to, I dared not deduce the purpose. Perhaps for fear of reprisal; if this is how they treated their own, then what would they do to an ungrateful outsider? A week later I found her in the refectory, laughing with another Sister. She greeted me cheerily. We spoke at length about her travels elsewhere. Of the garden walls, the latices, the tangled vines which sealed her alcove. She found a tunnel connecting to the cistern. She claimed to have crawled through it, and that they were waiting for her when she emerged. I was astounded by her lucidity-- but, more than that, her newfound resemblance to the Abbess. To the other Sisters which she dined with. A bleary haze to their eyes. Nearly blind. Nearly all of them. I observed then that I was surrounded by the dead.”*

## Gallu; the Risen Dead
The Gallu are humans who have been killed, and through mysterious means were resurrected as a demi-human. Every Gallu has a ‘curse’, which is an aggressive magical mutation that makes them appear bestial or inhuman. A Gallu’s curse is a Scar which has healed from the Major Wound that killed them. These curses are often influenced by the manner of death: a Gallu who was decapitated in life may have developed a plumage of feathers in a mantle over their torso, and a hoarse, haunting quality to their voice; a Gallu who died by drowning may have grown fishlike scales across their face and head, and a webbing between their fingers and toes; a Gallu who was speared through the spine might fold into a sharp hunch, with their skin becoming pallid and cracked like stone. Commonly, Gallu also develop a corpselike fog in the eyes.
## Becoming a Gallu
A player character may choose to become a Gallu at the moment of their death so long as at least half of their body remains to be reconstituted. Destroying the body prevents a character from becoming a Gallu. A new character may become a Gallu in their backstory by selecting the ‘Scarred’ Flaw. No matter when or how they die, a player who wants to turn their character into a Gallu must submit a Gallu Application or include it in their initial Character Application. This is to ensure a resurrection is still possible, to give GMs a chance to collaborate with the player, and to prevent lore-prohibited aesthetics.
## The Aftermath
A Gallu awakens from death within a week of having died, physically reformed but aggressively mutated from their brush with the other side. They gain no benefits besides the instant healing of all their wounds, and the Scarring of their lethal Major Wound. Scars healed before becoming a Gallu are not affected. It is possible for a Gallu to continue to resurrect when meeting their end assuming that the conditions are permitting. A Gallu who repeatedly reforms will resemble a human less and less until they are functionally unrecognizable, at which point they will fail to resurrect or lose all sapient thought.
